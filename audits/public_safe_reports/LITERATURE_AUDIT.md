# Q1089 primary-source comparison

Checked 2026-11-27. High confidence that the theorem answers the pinned positive-mass formulation. The exact v1 wording has a zero-weight ambiguity described below. No earlier solution was found in the bounded search. Publication priority remains unestablished.

- Lederman, *Incompleteness, Independence, and Negative Dominance*, arXiv:2311.08471v1, §3. Definitions 3.1–3.2 appear on printed/PDF pp. 23–24, Proposition 3.1 on p. 24, and Open Question 3.3 on p. 26. The question asks for simultaneous consistency of Good Expectations, Negative Dominance, Stochastic Dominance and Indifferent Independence. The author's papers page lists the work as a preprint. The arXiv record has one version submitted November 2023. The versioned PDF carries its own body date. [Versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08471v1#page=26) · [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08471) · [Author bibliography](https://harveylederman.com/papers.html)
- Definition 3.2 describes generalized lottery atoms with weights in [0,1], and its strict matched-pair clause does not explicitly exclude zero-weight atoms. The pinned catalog instead requires positive mass on strict pairs. Deleting zero-weight matched atoms before checking strictness gives the finite-support coupling formulation. If zero-weight pairs can witness strictness, adding one strictly ordered zero-weight pair lets any lottery strictly dominate itself, which is incompatible with a preorder's strict part. The theorem here uses the pinned positive-mass formulation, with no inference about what the literal ambiguity was intended to mean.
- Proposition 3.1 and Lemma 3.1 already give the three-axiom consistency argument using ordered extrema of good lotteries. The source itself says its minimal preorder does not identify the equal mixture of the horizontal and vertical mean-zero lotteries with the zero Dirac (p. 26). Taking each same-mean convex hull fixes that precise example and enforces the restricted mixture axiom in general. The proof separately checks strict cross-support witnesses in the enlarged transitive closure. Full Independence in Proposition 2.2 and continuity in other questions have distinct hypotheses. [Three-axiom result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08471v1#page=24)
- Tarsney, Lederman and Spears, *A Dominance Argument Against Incompleteness*, DOI 10.1215/00318108-11964820, uses a different ex ante Pareto and weak Negative Dominance setup. Bottomley and Williamson's discussion of Betweenness is a source cited by Lederman. No checked result from these papers constructs the particular four-axiom preorder here. [Tarsney–Lederman–Spears author copy](https://sites.utexas.edu/pwi/wp-content/uploads/sites/5951/2024/05/LTS_Incompleteness.pdf) · [Bottomley–Williamson](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/phpr.13006)
- Searches used the exact Question 3.3 wording, the four axiom names, the title and author, and same-mean convex hull phrasing. The contribution is a short modification of the source's construction on finite-support lotteries over product-ordered real vectors.
